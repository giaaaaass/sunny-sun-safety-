📊 Understanding UV levels

UV 3 or higher means sun protection is recommended, even if it is cloudy or cool.

0–2 · Low

Lower UV, but use normal sun-smart habits when outdoors for a long time.

3–5 · Moderate

Protect your skin: shade, clothing, hat, sunglasses and SPF 50+.

6–7 · High

Extra care is important. Reduce time in direct sun and use several forms of protection.

8–10 · Very high

Take extra precautions and avoid being in direct sun around the strongest UV hours where possible.

11+ · Extreme

Take every precaution. Stay in shade or indoors during peak UV where possible.

🧴 Your protection guide

Use broad-spectrum SPF 50+ sunscreen. Apply generously to exposed skin and reapply at least every 2 hours, and after swimming, sweating or towel-drying.

  • Wear a broad-brimmed hat, sunglasses and covering clothing.
  • Use shade, especially when UV is strongest.
  • Cloud and cooler temperatures do not guarantee low UV.
  • Follow sunscreen label directions and local sun-safety guidance.
Do not wait until your skin feels hot or looks red before protecting it. UV damage can happen before you notice symptoms. Redness, warmth, tenderness or discomfort can be signs of sunburn: get out of the sun, cool the skin gently and seek medical advice if symptoms are severe or worrying.

About this version

Sunny v0.2 is a working front-end prototype. UV forecasts use Open-Meteo. Places, profile, themes, journal, habits and demo friend data are stored locally in this browser. Online friend connections, cross-device syncing, real push reminders and native widgets need additional services or a native iPhone app.

Sunny is a sun-protection companion, not a medical service.
